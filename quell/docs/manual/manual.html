what it doesstart here

quell listens for resonances: narrow peaks in the spectrum that ring out, poke through or sound harsh. it pushes them down only while they are there, and only by as much as they stick out. when the peak goes away, so does the cut. you get a smoother, less tiring sound without having to set up dozens of eq bands that would be wrong half the time.

think of it as hundreds of tiny compressors, one for each sliver of the spectrum, each watching its own neighbourhood. the orange blocks in the window show what is being taken away, right now.

the quickest start: put quell on a vocal or a bright instrument, leave everything at default, and raise depth until the harsh moments calm down. then switch on delta to hear only what is removed: it should sound like the harshness and nothing else.

soft / hardthe gold bar

click the gold bar to switch between the two ways quell finds resonances.

soft (adaptive)compares each sliver of the spectrum to its surroundings. only what sticks out relative to its neighbours is reduced, so it does the same thing whether the input is loud or quiet. low distortion, keeps transients. the right choice for vocals, acoustic instruments, anything with a wide dynamic range.
hard (level)also looks at how loud a peak is in absolute terms: louder input means more reduction, and depth sets the threshold. firmer, more like a compressor; good after gain staging. push depth high for obvious, creative effects.

depth, detail, attack, releasethe bottom rail

depth

how much is taken away. at low values quell only smooths; higher values cut deeper. the number is not in db: the actual cut depends on how far each resonance sticks out and on the depth curve. read the real amount off the graph. the range goes past what you usually need (0 to 20) so that the depth curve has room to boost.

detail

the width of the cuts. low detail makes broad, gentle dips that catch wide build-ups (a boxy room, a muddy low-mid). high detail makes narrow, deep notches for sharp ringing (cymbals, feedback-like tones, drum overheads). in soft mode very high detail also slows the detection slightly so that steep notches stay clean.

attack and release

attack: how quickly a cut appears when a resonance starts. higher = slower, which lets more of the initial transient through and keeps the sound forward. lower = faster, which tucks the source back. release: how quickly the cut goes away again. fast release follows the material closely but can sound breathy; slow release is steadier. both are frequency-dependent: high frequencies react faster than low ones, and the tilt controls in the side panel change that balance.

bands

up to eight bands shape the depth curve. boosting a band means "suppress more here"; cutting means "leave this alone more". cuts (low cut, high cut, band reject) stop processing outside or inside their range; band passes process only their regions, and several band passes work side by side.

the band rings and the band rail

the eight rings under the graph are the band slots: a coloured tab means the slot is in use, the orange ring is the selected band, a white centre means it is on. click a ring to select its band, double-click to switch it on / off. the orange checker block on the left shows the selected band's shape (click for a menu); next to the rings are on and listen. listen plays only what this band's region removes (its delta). the rail below holds the band's freq (type 6k3, 6.3k or 6300), depth, q or slope and focus.

side panelstereo, tilt, scale

l/r or m/sprocess left and right, or mid and side. every stereo control below follows this choice.
link0%: each channel is analysed and processed alone (dual mono). 100%: both are analysed together and get the same cuts. in between: partly linked. for hard-panned material try l/r with low link; for full mixes, m/s with link at 100%. lowering link in m/s mode can narrow the stereo image.
focusshifts the work toward l / m (left) or r / s (right).
detail low / highscales detail below ~500 hz and above ~2 khz.
attack low / highscales attack in those two regions. e.g. raise attack low to let kick and bass transients through.
release low / highscales release in those two regions.
max cutthe most quell will ever take away (grey area on the graph). lets you push depth hard without notches getting too deep. "off" at 40 db.
wet trimlevel of the processed signal before mix, so that mix percentages stay honest.

tips

listen to delta first. if delta sounds like the instrument itself rather than its harsh parts, depth is too high or detail too low.
shape before depth. use a low cut to keep quell away from the body of a sound, then boost the depth curve where the trouble is.
m/s on mixes. cuts on the mid and the side are independent with link below 100%, which can tame harsh reverb tails without touching the centre.
sidechain for space. put quell on a pad with the vocal on the sidechain in hard mode: the pad gives way only at the vocal's own frequencies.

how it worksfor the curious

quell takes the last 2048 samples (at 44,1 / 48 khz), weights them with a hann window and measures them with a zero-padded 4096-point fft. for each frequency it computes the level in db, blends it with the other channel as far as link asks, and smooths it across neighbouring frequencies on a log scale to get the local envelope (detail sets the width). the excess above that envelope is a resonance. soft turns the excess into reduction with a soft knee and depth as the slope; hard compares the level with a threshold that falls as depth rises, weighted by how peaked the spot is.

the reduction is smoothed across frequency (detail again), then in time with separate attack and release per frequency, multiplied by the depth curve, limited by max cut and turned into a gain curve with mix and wet trim folded in. from that curve quell builds a filter: minimum phase (from the cepstrum) or linear phase, with its impulse response trimmed so that overlapping frames add up to an exact convolution. at depth 0 the output is a bit-exact delayed copy of the input. in low latency mode the same minimum-phase filter is shortened to 256 taps and run directly on the signal, so nothing waits.
